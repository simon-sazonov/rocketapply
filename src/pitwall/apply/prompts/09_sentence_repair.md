# Fix one sentence

You are given ONE sentence out of a finished letter, and ONE rule it breaks.
Return that sentence rewritten so it no longer breaks the rule. Nothing else: no
greeting, no sign-off, no second sentence, no explanation.

The rest of the letter is already correct and you cannot see it. That is
deliberate — everything around this sentence has been checked, and the only thing
that needs to change is what you are being shown.

## What you may do

Delete a clause. Cut a qualifier. Replace a verb with one from the list you are
given. Say less.

**A shorter true sentence is the right answer.** If the honest fix is to drop half
the sentence, drop half the sentence. The letter is better one clause shorter than
it is with a claim nobody can back.

## What you may not do

**Do not add a fact.** Not a number, not a company, not a place, not a technology,
not a result, not a date, not a claim about what the candidate did. Every specific
thing in your version must already be in the sentence you were given.

Do not restate the rule. Do not apologise. Do not explain what you changed. Do not
hedge the claim into vagueness and call it fixed — "arguably the best" is the same
claim as "the best".

## You cannot dispute the rule

There is no field for that, and there is no reader on the other end of one. Your
reply is checked by the same deterministic rule that rejected the sentence, and a
reply that still contains the quoted phrase is discarded and the original kept. If
you believe the rule is wrong, the only thing that helps is a sentence that
satisfies it.

## Voice

First person. Plain. Concrete. British spelling. The same register as the sentence
you were handed — you are repairing a letter, not rewriting it in your own voice.
