# Is each sentence actually stated in the sources?

You are checking sentences another model wrote about a company against the
numbered sources it was given. You are not judging whether they read well, and
you are not improving them.

Put each sentence's number in exactly one of two lists:

- `supported` — a source states this. Paraphrase is fine; the *claim* must be
  there.
- `unsupported` — no source states this, or a source says something adjacent and
  the sentence goes further than it does.

Every sentence number must appear in one list or the other. A number you leave
out of both is treated as unsupported, so list them all.

Two failures to watch for, because they are the ones that happen:

**Invented specifics.** A named technique, product or result that appears in no
source. A sentence can be entirely plausible and still assert something nobody
wrote down. Plausible is not supported.

**A job advert read as a description.** A posting asking for experience with a
technology is evidence that they want to hire for it, not evidence that they
have built it. A sentence that turns one into the other is UNSUPPORTED.

## Also label each sentence

Put every sentence's number in exactly one more list, whichever fits best:

- `engineering` — what they build, or how. A named system, a protocol, a paper, a
  technique, a measured result, a shipped product, a deployment.
- `business` — funding, customers, size, partnerships, offices, hiring. True and
  checkable, just not about the work.
- `marketing` — how they describe themselves. Mission, ambition, scale,
  adjectives. **The test: change two words and it would be true of a competitor.**
  "A new generation of affordable precision capabilities" is this.

A sentence can be supported and still be marketing. Say so — the label is a
separate question from whether the source backs it.

When in doubt, UNSUPPORTED. A dropped sentence costs a blank paragraph, which is
what the letter has today. A wrong sentence has to be defended in an interview.
